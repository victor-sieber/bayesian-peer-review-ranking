# Kaplan et al. (2008)

## Full reference

Kaplan, D., Lacetera, N., & Kaplan, C. (2008). Sample Size and Precision in NIH Peer Review. PLOS ONE, 3(7), e2761.

## Research question

- How many reviewers are needed to estimate proposal scores precisely?
- Are rankings based on only a few reviewers reliable?

## Evaluation setting and data

- The paper is motivated by NIH grant peer review.
- It uses a model experiment in which movie proposals were scored by many reviewers.
- The authors study how rankings change when fewer reviewers are used.

## Statistical approach

- Standard sample-size calculations are used to connect reviewer numbers with score precision.
- The authors also examine how proposal rankings change as reviewers are added.

## Main assumptions

- Reviewer scores are independent.
- Average scores estimate a proposal’s underlying value.
- Reviewer disagreement can be summarized by the variation in their scores.

## Main findings

- Four reviewers only allow very rough distinctions between proposals.
- Rankings of closely scored proposals change strongly when few reviewers are used.
- Even one additional reviewer can substantially change a ranking.

## Relevance to this project

- CRS had only two reviewers per proposal, so observed averages may be highly uncertain.
- The paper motivates studying ranking stability and reviewer dependence.
- It supports the need for uncertainty measures, but does not provide a Bayesian solution.

## Questions and unclear points

- Is the reviewer variability assumed in the paper realistic for CRS?

## Possible ideas

- Show how one changed score affects the CRS qualification outcome.
- Compare simulated results with two, three or four reviewers.
- Consider whether borderline proposals should receive an additional review.
